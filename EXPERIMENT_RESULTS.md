# Experiment results and final model selection

> Generated by `python -m backend.benchmarking.make_report` from the stored benchmark artifacts; every table cell is read from a file in `storage/`. Narrative conclusions are written by hand and are limited to what those numbers support. **All retrieval labels are synthetic and not human-verified** (see section 2); read the numbers as indicative, not as ground truth.

## 1. Experimental setup
- Hardware/software: CPU only (16 logical cores, no GPU), Python 3.13, torch 2.10, sentence-transformers 5.2, FAISS 1.13, scikit-learn 1.6. Fixed seed 42 for sampling, splits, bootstrap and model training.
- Corpus: the 49 PDFs in `data/`; 46 were processed into 1961 chunks (`recursive-256-15`); 3 are truncated downloads that no extractor can open (logged as extraction failures).
- **Corpus snapshots.** An early bug (title de-duplication trusted a journal-template title) had silently dropped one paper, so the original benchmark ran on 48 papers / 1,878 chunks. After fixing it the paper was added and the core evaluation (selection run `run0004`, the dev/validation/test tables in section 6, and the error analysis in section 8) was re-run on the **49-paper / 1,961-chunk** corpus. The supporting studies (hybrid tuning, chunk sweep, reranker, lexical-bias probe, label audit, relevance classifier, evidence-gate calibration, context tuning) and the efficiency measurements ran on the earlier 48-paper snapshot; adding 4% more text is unlikely to change them materially, but they were not repeated.
- PDF extraction (measured on the corpus): PyMuPDF 0.013 s/page and 3.41 of 4 core sections found; pdfplumber 0.284 s/page and 2.57. PyMuPDF is primary, pdfplumber the fallback.
- Protocol: hybrid fusion weights tuned on the **dev** split; the model is **selected on validation**; the **test** split was evaluated once afterwards and only reported.

## 2. Dataset
- Version `abb1ed6844`: 101 queries generated with a local Qwen2.5-1.5B-Instruct (greedy decoding), automatically validated (form, length, copy filter, answerability score), then **89 retained** after excluding 12 context-dependent queries (decision made before any model result was seen). Target was >= 100; this is below it.
- Splits are by paper (no paper appears in two splits): 27/9/9 papers; queries dev 44, validation 21, test 24.
- Categories: definition 13, methodology 14, experimental results 15, comparative 16, technical 16, cross paper 15. Human-verified: **0**. Relevance is stored as evidence text and mapped onto any chunking by shingle coverage (grade 2 >= 50% covered, grade 1 >= 10%).
- Known biases: labels are **incomplete** (one source passage per query; an AI review of 24 rank-1 results found 8 genuinely relevant but unlabeled, spread across all models); queries **share vocabulary** with their source passage (mean 57% of content words), which favours lexical retrieval; samples are small (validation 21, test 24).

## 5. Evaluation methodology
- Ranking metrics at K = 1, 3, 5, 10: Precision, Recall (relative to all relevant chunks), nDCG (graded), MRR@10, MAP (AP@10). ROC-AUC, PR-AUC, F1 and accuracy are **not** used for the retrieval models (no binary decision exists); they are reported only for the relevance classifier (section 10).
- Selection (`backend/benchmarking/selection.py`, no hand-set weights): feasibility (nDCG@10 >= 0.5, Recall@5 >= 0.4, health check, memory <= 25% of available RAM, p95 <= 500 ms, >= 2.0 qps, re-index <= 7200 s) -> highest nDCG@10 is the reference -> another model replaces it only if the lower bound of a 95% paired-bootstrap interval of the nDCG@10 difference is >= -0.02 -> smallest geometric-mean resource footprint among the tied.
- Efficiency: isolated process per model, 89 queries x 3 rounds, sequential single client. Dense indexing time is extrapolated from a 192-chunk sample; TF-IDF is measured.

## 8. Error analysis (all 89 retained queries, top-10 outcomes; `backend/benchmarking/error_analysis.py`)
| Model | relevant chunk ranked 1st | ranked 2-5 | ranked 6-10 | right paper, wrong passage | right paper not retrieved |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| TF-IDF | 65 | 21 | 3 | 0 | 0 |
| SBERT (MiniLM) | 49 | 31 | 2 | 5 | 2 |
| E5-base | 55 | 28 | 2 | 3 | 1 |
| BGE-base | 58 | 23 | 4 | 3 | 1 |
| Hybrid TF-IDF+E5 | 68 | 20 | 1 | 0 | 0 |

- TF-IDF and the hybrid always retrieve the right paper in the top 10 (0 misses); their remaining error is **ranking within the top 10**. Dense-only models fully miss a few queries (SBERT 7, E5 4, BGE 4).
- **Cross-paper questions are a weakness.** For the 15 cross-paper queries, TF-IDF and the hybrid retrieve exactly one of the two relevant papers in the top 10 every time ({'1_of_2_papers': 15}); dense-only models cover both in 2-3 cases. Part of this is the label problem (other papers on the same topic may also be valid), part is retrieval recall.
- The RAG context contained both relevant papers for 2 of 15 unscoped cross-paper queries. A tuning study on dev+validation (depth and diversification over the best N papers) found no gain worth adopting: adopted = False; on test the rate was 0.20 before and 0.20 after (n = 5). The limit is retrieval recall, not context selection.
- **Bug found by this analysis:** when the user *selects* several papers, a single filtered top-20 could be dominated by one paper, silently dropping the others (both selected papers reached the context in only 8 of 15 cases). Retrieval is now done per selected paper and interleaved: **15/15**. Selecting papers is therefore the reliable route for comparisons.

## 10. Supporting studies
- **Reranker** (BAAI/bge-reranker-base, top-20): validation change +0.050 (95% CI [-0.015, +0.123]); rule fixed in advance (adopt only if the lower bound is above 0) -> **not adopted**; about 6.51 s/query on CPU.
- **Lexical-bias probe**: rewording queries cut query-passage word overlap from 0.61 to 0.44; nDCG@10 fell most for TF-IDF (by 0.108) and least for SBERT (by 0.040); the hybrid was best on reworded queries (0.651, paired p = 0.0012 against TF-IDF). The original queries favour lexical matching.
- **Label-completeness audit** (AI reviewer, rank-1 unlabeled results only, validation): adding 8 judged-relevant passages raised every model by about 0.02-0.03 nDCG@10 and did not change the ranking (TF-IDF 0.747, SBERT 0.586, E5-base 0.648, BGE-base 0.685, Hybrid 0.769).
- **Relevance classifier for SHAP/LIME** (logreg, chosen by validation PR-AUC): ROC-AUC 0.888 / 0.856, PR-AUC 0.623 / 0.546, F1 0.571 / 0.515 (validation / test; positive rate ~11%). Not used for ranking (no significant gain).
- **RAG evidence gate** (dense cosine >= 0.8225): kept 100% of validation and 95.8% of test in-domain queries and refused 100% of held-out off-topic questions (30 clearly out-of-domain questions written by the assistant; near-domain unanswerable questions were not tested).

## 11. Final model selection
**Production: Hybrid TF-IDF+E5** (registry run `run0004`, selected on the validation split). **Fallback: TF-IDF** (cheapest feasible model, independent of any neural model).

Why: the hybrid has the highest validation nDCG@10 (0.742) and TF-IDF could not be *shown* to be non-inferior (-0.042, 95% CI [-0.096, +0.002] against a margin of -0.02); the dense-only models are worse on validation and test (SBERT, E5 and BGE are each significantly worse than the hybrid on test at p < 0.05, uncorrected for multiple comparisons) and cost more latency (E5/BGE about 33x TF-IDF's, SBERT about 8x). Honest reading: **the hybrid's advantage over TF-IDF alone is small and not statistically significant on validation or test**; it was selected because the evidence could not rule out a material gap with this little data, and the lexical-bias probe supports it. If more or human-verified queries show TF-IDF is equivalent, the selection logic will switch to it automatically (it is 30x cheaper). Dense-only retrieval (SBERT, E5, BGE) is not justified on this corpus and these labels.

## 12. Limitations, and the additional evaluation that is required
1. **No human-verified labels.** The most important missing piece. Review `storage/benchmark/review.csv` (101 queries) and re-run the benchmark; with 100+ verified queries the decision between TF-IDF and the hybrid can be made with confidence.
2. **Dataset size and bias**: 89 queries (target >= 100), lexical overlap with source passages, incomplete labels, 21/24 queries in validation/test.
3. **Generation quality is not evaluated.** Faithfulness, answer relevance, context precision/recall, groundedness, citation correctness against human judgement, RAGAS-style judging, and cost per query were not measured because no LLM was available (OpenAI account without credits; the 1.5B local model ignores citation instructions). Only lexical citation heuristics exist. This needs a working LLM and a manually validated subset.
4. Only one chunking configuration was used for E5/BGE/hybrid; the sweep covered TF-IDF and SBERT only. Semantic chunking was not benchmarked.
5. Efficiency numbers are CPU-only; dense indexing times are extrapolated from a sample.
6. Cross-paper retrieval is weak without explicit paper selection (section 8).
7. Metadata: papers added as plain PDFs have no authors/years; zero-shot topic labels are unvalidated; scanned PDFs are not OCR'd; 3 of 49 PDFs in `data/` are corrupt.
8. The Docker image was not built (daemon unavailable); see DEPLOYMENT.md.

**Future improvements:** human-reviewed query set; a credited LLM plus a generation-evaluation harness; query decomposition and per-topic retrieval for cross-paper questions; a stronger sparse baseline (BM25) as a hybrid partner; reranker re-test with more queries; GPU for the larger embedding models; a larger, multi-domain corpus.
